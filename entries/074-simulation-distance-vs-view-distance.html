Trimming view-distance was supposed to fix lag, but broke farms instead

A survival server under heavy load had view-distance turned down from 10 to 6 in server.properties, hoping to cut CPU/memory use. It did help a little, but the real effect wasn't what was expected: mob farms and redstone contraptions sitting a bit outside the base stopped working, even though nobody had moved or touched them. Players standing right next to their own farm saw it running fine; anything a bit further out just went quiet.

Why: view-distance is about rendering, not simulation

view-distance only controls how many chunks the server sends to a client to draw — it's a purely visual/network setting, the server-side equivalent of a client's own render-distance slider. The setting that actually decides how far around a player the world keeps ticking — mob AI, redstone, crop growth, hoppers, block ticks — is a separate one: simulation-distance. Chunks inside the simulation radius get fully processed every tick regardless of whether they're also being rendered to anyone; chunks outside it are frozen in place. Turning down view-distance alone does nothing to that radius — but on some server distributions simulation-distance defaults to tracking the view-distance value unless it's set explicitly, so a well-meaning render-distance cut can silently shrink the simulation radius along with it. 

# server.properties
view-distance=10
simulation-distance=10   # set independently — don't assume it follows view-distance

The actual lesson

On a Minecraft server, "how far players can see" and "how far the world is actually alive" are two different radii with two different settings. When tuning performance, cut whichever one is actually causing the cost — usually simulation-distance, since ticking entities and redstone is far more expensive than streaming chunk data — and always check both values after changing either one, rather than assuming a single setting covers both jobs.
